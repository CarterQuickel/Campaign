[Part 9] - The Gaping Serpent

[The abandoned ascent]

The Gaping Serpent is unlike the other creatures encountered on the mountain.

Supaida is a hunter. The Red-Maned Wolf is a territorial predator. The Gaping Serpent is something ancient, a creature that has survived in the mountain since long before Haldritch was founded.

The scouts believe the serpent has begun moving closer to the surface because something beneath the mountain has changed.

The hero returns to the upper pass and follows the missing expedition's trail.

The first signs of the serpent are subtle. Trees have been pushed aside, boulders have been dragged from their resting places, and the mountain's stone is marked by enormous grooves.

Eventually, the hero finds a survey camp that has been destroyed.

There are no bodies. The tents have been crushed, equipment has been scattered, and the rock walls bear deep scratches. An abandoned journal lies beneath a broken supply crate.

Its final entry reads:

We thought the mountain was moving. Then the ridge opened its eyes.

The trail continues toward a narrow passage where the stone walls are covered in enormous discarded scales.
Some are as large as shields.

Others are embedded in the rock as if the creature has been scraping against the walls for centuries.

The hero realizes that the serpent has shed its skin repeatedly throughout its lifetime. Its discarded scales have accumulated across the mountain, creating a false impression that the creature has died and been replaced.

But there is only one serpent.

And it is still growing.

[The royal research station]

Beyond the shed scales, the hero discovers an abandoned research station bearing the royal insignia of Haldritch.

This facility was established after the king began investigating the mountain's unusual energy. The researchers were searching for ways to strengthen Alternate X, hoping that whatever made the serpent so resilient could be reproduced.

The hero finds research documents describing a strange energy source buried deep beneath the mountain.

The researchers believed that the Gaping Serpent had survived for centuries by adapting to this energy. Its scales had become unnaturally durable, its body could withstand enormous injuries, and its senses allowed it to detect movement through vibrations in the surrounding stone.

The researchers also observed something disturbing.

The energy beneath the mountain seemed to react to the serpent's presence.

When the creature moved, the energy fluctuated. When it was injured, the fluctuations became violent. Some researchers believed the serpent was absorbing the energy; others believed it was containing something beneath the mountain.

No conclusion was reached.

The final reports reveal that the king ordered the research station abandoned after several expeditions disappeared.
He feared that the serpent might become another uncontrollable threat.

Even then, he continued searching for ways to exploit its biology.

The hero now understands why the serpent matters to the story: the king's attempts to prevent the Forthcoming led him to interfere with something he never fully understood.

The hero continues downward.

The passage opens into an immense cavern.

At its center lies a seemingly bottomless abyss.

The walls are covered in ancient markings, some of which predate the kingdom. The remains of an old stone structure stand near the edge, partially swallowed by the earth.

The cavern is unnaturally quiet.

Then the ground moves.

What the hero thought was a ridge of black stone begins to rise. A massive coil shifts against the cavern wall, scattering rocks into the darkness below.

Two enormous eyes open.

The Gaping Serpent has awakened.

[The Gaping Maw]

The Gaping Serpent is a colossal snake whose body fills much of the cavern. Its scales are blackened by age, with faint veins of supernatural light visible beneath them.

Its head is large enough to swallow a person whole. Its jaws open far wider than a natural creature's should, revealing rows of teeth and an unnaturally deep throat.

Its name comes from the sight of that impossible mouth.

The serpent does not speak, bargain, or explain its purpose. It reacts to the hero as an intruder who has entered a territory it has guarded for ages.

The cavern becomes the battlefield.

[Boss phase one] — The Stalking Coil

The serpent circles the arena, striking from unexpected angles. Its body moves along the walls and across the cavern floor, making it difficult to determine where the next attack will come from.

Its tail smashes pillars and collapses sections of the arena, gradually reducing the hero's available space.
The hero must observe its movements and learn to anticipate its strikes.

[Boss phase two] — The Gaping Maw

As the serpent becomes injured, it begins opening its enormous jaws and attempting to swallow the hero whole.
Its lunges are devastating, and its movements create powerful gusts that pull loose debris toward its mouth. A direct hit can send the hero crashing across the cavern.

The hero must use the terrain to evade its attacks and exploit the brief moments when the serpent's jaws remain open.

[Boss phase three] — The False Dawn

At low health, the serpent's scales begin to glow.

The energy beneath the mountain surges through its body, revealing the same unnatural radiance described in the royal research records. Its armor begins cracking, exposing vulnerable areas beneath the scales.

The cavern trembles. Ancient markings illuminate across the walls, and the abyss below begins to emit a faint, impossible light.

The serpent becomes more aggressive, but its connection to the energy is also weakening. The hero must strike its exposed areas while avoiding the increasingly unstable terrain.

Eventually, the Gaping Serpent releases one final hiss.

Its enormous body collapses beside the abyss.

For the first time in centuries, the cavern falls silent.

What the serpent was guarding

The hero approaches the serpent's remains.

The glow beneath its scales fades, but the energy below the mountain does not disappear. Instead, it becomes quieter, as if something has stopped disturbing it.

Near the serpent's nest, the hero discovers a hidden chamber containing the remains of an older civilization. The stonework is far older than Haldritch, and its markings resemble symbols found in the Oracle's records.
The serpent was not created by the king.

It was not an experiment.

It had been here long before Haldritch existed.

The royal researchers came to the mountain because they believed they could reproduce its resilience. They never understood whether the serpent was feeding on the energy beneath the mountain or preventing that energy from escaping.

Now that the serpent is dead, the hero cannot know for certain what consequences its death will bring.

Among the abandoned equipment, the hero finds a rare crystal: Fractured Fate.

The crystal contains a fragment of a future that appears never to have occurred. When the hero holds it near the ancient markings, the surface briefly reflects an image of Haldritch before its destruction.

The image changes.

The hero sees the palace burning, the king standing among the ruins, and a massive silhouette moving behind him.
Then the vision ends.

The hero has found the item needed by the reforger at the Scouts' Camp. But the crystal's appearance also suggests that the serpent's chamber and the Forthcoming are connected in ways the king never understood.

The hero returns to the camp with Fractured Fate.

[The consequences of killing the serpent]

The Gaping Serpent's death should have consequences beyond its boss reward.

After the hero returns, scouts report strange changes in the mountain. Tremors have become less frequent, but distant lights now appear in places where there were none before.

Some believe the serpent was the source of the mountain's disturbances.

Others believe it had been holding something back.

The hero has no definitive answer.

This uncertainty gives the player a reason to continue investigating rather than treating the serpent's death as the resolution of the mountain's mystery.
