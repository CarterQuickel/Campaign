[Part 10] - The King’s Hidden Chamber

[The Crown's Key]

After defeating Supaida, the Red-Maned Wolf, and the Gaping Serpent, the hero returns to the Scouts' Camp to recover.

The mountain has revealed disturbing connections between Haldritch's royal research and the strange forces gathering across the land. The Gaping Serpent's cavern contained ancient markings and evidence of unusual energy beneath the mountain, while the abandoned research station showed that the king had attempted to exploit that energy.

The hero now has a reason to investigate the palace more thoroughly.

Clara's earlier gift, the Crown of Haldritch, may provide the means to do so.

The hero returns to the Fallen Palace and makes their way to the throne room where they defeated the Fallen King. Behind the throne lies an entrance concealed by rubble and damaged stonework.

At its center is an ancient locking mechanism bearing the royal insignia.

When the hero places the crown into the mechanism, the palace responds. Hidden gears turn beneath the floor, and the stone doors slowly open, revealing a staircase descending into darkness.

The King's Hidden Chambers have remained sealed since the fall of Haldritch.

Whatever lies within may explain how the king went from a ruler attempting to save his people to the man responsible for destroying his own kingdom.

[The King's Private Records]

The hidden chambers are remarkably well preserved compared with the rest of the palace.

Unlike the public halls above, these rooms were accessible only to the king and a select group of royal advisers. Shelves contain sealed documents, maps of the mountain, and records of experiments conducted in the dungeons.

The hero discovers correspondence showing that the king became increasingly obsessed with the Oracle's prophecy.

At first, he attempted to prepare Haldritch for the predicted catastrophe. He ordered supplies stored, defenses strengthened, and expeditions sent to investigate unusual phenomena beyond the kingdom.

But as the Oracle's warnings became more frequent, the king began to fear that ordinary preparations would never be enough.

He became convinced that humanity required a weapon capable of defeating the Forthcoming.

That belief led to the creation of Alternate X.

The records show the gradual transformation of the king's priorities. Advisers who questioned his experiments were dismissed. Researchers were pressured to continue their work despite mounting failures. Resources were diverted away from the kingdom's population and into increasingly dangerous projects.

The king did not begin as a monster.

He became one while trying to prevent something worse.

[The Experiment Beneath the Palace]

Deeper within the chambers, the hero discovers a sealed laboratory.

Its containment rooms are damaged, their doors bent outward. Several glass chambers have been shattered, and heavy restraints lie broken across the floor.

This is where Alternate X was developed.

The hero finds research logs describing the creature's construction. It was designed to possess extraordinary physical strength, an almost impenetrable exoskeleton, and heightened senses capable of detecting threats beyond ordinary human perception.

The researchers believed these abilities would allow it to confront the Forthcoming when the entity finally appeared.
However, the experiments produced troubling results.

Alternate X became increasingly aggressive and difficult to control. It reacted violently to certain supernatural energies, sometimes attacking empty spaces or turning toward threats that the researchers could not detect.

The king ordered the research to continue.

A final report, written shortly before the creature escaped, contains a disturbing observation:

The subject appears to recognize the approaching phenomenon. We cannot determine whether this response is instinctive, learned, or evidence of something more.

The hero realizes that Alternate X may have been reacting to the Forthcoming long before anyone else could perceive its arrival.

But the laboratory contains another mystery.

The king's records reference a powerful frequency capable of disrupting Alternate X's enhanced hearing. The royal researchers commissioned the Withstanders and their mystical gongs to exploit this weakness in the event of a rebellion.

The hero now understands why those creatures were found in the mountain caves.

They were part of the king's contingency plans.

[The Last Warning]

At the deepest point of the laboratory, the hero discovers the king's final personal journal.

Its pages describe the days immediately preceding Alternate X's escape.

The king writes that the Oracle's visions had begun changing. The predicted catastrophe no longer appeared as a distant event, but as something drawing closer with every passing day.

He feared that he had run out of time.

In his final entries, he describes hearing strange sounds throughout the palace and seeing an unnatural golden light beyond the windows. He believed the Forthcoming was already influencing the world.

His response was to tighten containment procedures and demand that Alternate X be prepared for deployment.

The final entry ends abruptly.

If the Forthcoming comes before the subject is ready, then everything we have sacrificed will have been for nothing.
The hero closes the journal.

The king had known that the catastrophe was approaching. He had devoted his kingdom to stopping it, but his desperation had prevented him from recognizing the consequences of his own actions.

The hidden chambers contain no definitive explanation of what the Forthcoming truly is.

They provide something more useful: evidence that the king's greatest fear was real, and that Alternate X was created specifically to confront it.

The hero leaves the palace with a clearer understanding of Haldritch's fall.

But the golden light beginning to appear on the horizon suggests that the king's deadline has finally arrived.
